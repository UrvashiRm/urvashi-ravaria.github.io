Algorithmic Personalization & Online Consumer Purchase Intention

A quantitative dissertation study measuring how algorithm-driven recommendations influence online consumer purchase intention, using a Usage & Attitude framework and PLS-SEM analysis on a 200-respondent dataset.

The question

Online platforms increasingly rely on algorithmic recommendations to drive purchase decisions. This study set out to test which factors of that experience — relevance, trust, perceived personalization, among others — actually move a consumer from browsing to buying, using a structural equation model built on survey data.

Catching my own errors

Partway through analysis, I ran a full correction pass on the model and found statistical errors in the original run. Fixing them changed the results meaningfully — one hypothesis that had shown as supported (H4) flipped to not supported once the model was corrected. I also re-audited all 23 source citations against the original PDFs to confirm every reference was accurately attributed before final submission.

Full methodology, hypothesis structure, and results are documented in the dissertation report, available on request.
