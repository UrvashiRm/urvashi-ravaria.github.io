Leaf Cutlery: Demand & Behaviour Analytics Dashboard

A decision-support dashboard for a sustainable disposable-cutlery brand, built from a 2,000-respondent survey to answer one question: with a limited marketing budget, who should they target, and how should they plan for growth?

This was a four-person group project running the full analytics lifecycle — exploration, segmentation, prediction, association rules, and forecasting. I owned three sections: market basket analysis, demand forecasting, and the live customer-scoring tool.

The problem I found and fixed

The first version of the market basket analysis ran on a single product-choice column. Since each customer only picks one product, every "basket" had exactly one item, so no co-occurrence could ever be found — it was structurally broken. I rebuilt it by encoding each customer as a basket of behaviours instead (eco-awareness, online habit, spend tier, event frequency, product, intent), so the association rules actually meant something. The rules now surface a real, targetable audience: customers who host events frequently and are highly eco-aware convert well above baseline.

Forecasting for planning, not just prediction

The survey was a single snapshot with no time dimension, so I built a monthly order series with growth trend and event-driven seasonality, then applied three forecasting methods — moving average, linear trend, and Holt's exponential smoothing. All three agreed on direction: upward, with peaks around event-heavy months. That let us recommend the trend line for capacity planning and the moving average as a conservative floor for procurement, so the business neither overstocks nor runs out during peak season.

Turning analysis into a usable tool

The rest of the dashboard produces insight; this tab operationalises it. A sales or marketing person enters a prospect's profile and the tool returns a purchase probability plus a recommendation — prioritise, nurture, or minimal spend. This was the piece that made the whole project feel like a product rather than a report.

I also caught a second broken analysis in the original build — a regression model run on a yes/no outcome, which is statistically meaningless — and rebuilt it as a proper spend-prediction model. Full write-up and dataset available on request.
